The problem

Every tokenised asset has a holder problem.

Securities rules, AML law and licence conditions say the same thing: know who holds the asset, at issuance and every day after. On chain, that question has three bad answers today.

Per-app KYC
Rebuild everything
Vendor contract, PII flow, evidence retention and rescreening repeat for every asset and every chain.
Reusable boolean
“KYC passed”
Hides what was checked, by whom, under which regime, how long ago, and whether it is still true.
Cross-chain copy
Trust the relayer
A signing key becomes the compliance truth. The trust problem does not go away; it moves.
Same credential, asked byWhat the asset actually needs to knowAnswer today
KR production RWA noteDocument authenticity · bank account · sanctions · production regime · KR jurisdiction · trusted issuer · under 30 days oldnot expressible
KR pilot noteSame checks, sandbox regime accepted, seven-day freshnessnot expressible
Any note, next monthWas this holder revoked since? By whom? Can the asset find out without an admin key?no
What is missing is a policy layer the asset owns: what was checked, by whom, under which regime, how recently, and whether it is still standing.
Proofmark02
The product

Verify. Prove. Enforce.

Proofmark separates what an issuer checked from whether that is enough for this asset, and makes the second question a view function on Creditcoin.

1 · Verify
Credential in
Wallet signature, ID document, bank account, sanctions. Only checks that ran set a bit. Korean rails are integrated in code; the public sandbox labels its demo adapters on the page, in the evidence and on chain.
method bits regime assurance
2 · Prove
Attestcoin
The issuer emits on Ethereum. Creditcoin's BlockProver verifies that exact source transaction inside our ASC. No Proofmark oracle, no relayer signature, nothing to trust in the middle.
source proof ordered replay-safe
3 · Enforce
Frozen policy
Each asset binds to a frozen policy: methods, assurance, age, regime, jurisdiction, issuer, roster freshness. isVerified(wallet, policy) answers; the note's transfer hook enforces it on both sides.
isVerified RWA gate revocation
Positioning: the compliance gateway for Creditcoin. One layer above credential providers, one call below every asset.

Proofmark04
The insight, as a return value

One mark. Two policies. Two answers.

Policy #1 · KR productionisVerified = false
methods 0x10024 · assurance ≥ 2 · max age 30 d
regime 1 production · jurisdiction 410 · pinned issuer · frozen
Policy #2 · KR sandbox pilotisVerified = true
methods 0x10024 · assurance ≥ 2 · max age 7 d
regime 2 sandbox · jurisdiction 410 · pinned issuer · frozen
The same wallet, the same mark, read by two frozen policies on CC3. The mark honestly carries regime = sandbox, so production says no and the pilot says yes. A note binds to one policy for life. Nobody, including us, can loosen an asset's rules after the fact. Portability is a view function, not a promise.

Proofmark08
Why Creditcoin · why now

Creditcoin becomes the compliance decision layer for credentials issued anywhere.

For assets
Every RWA, lending and stablecoin launch on Creditcoin repeats the same holder-eligibility work. One frozen policy and one read replace it. Reads stay free and permissionless.
For issuers
One Creditcoin integration exposes an existing credential to every application and policy on the chain. Issuers keep their customer; Proofmark carries the fact.
For the protocol
A product that stops working without Attestcoin: proven source events replace the operator signature that otherwise owns cross-chain compliance.
No top-down TAM. The first market is two integrations: one Creditcoin asset that consumes a policy and one issuer that supplies a credential. Everything after that is repetition.
